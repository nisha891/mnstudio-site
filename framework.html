Our framework

The Debt Triangle Shrinking Framework.

The product was working. Now every release takes longer, every fix breaks something else, and the team is tired in a way one good sprint won’t fix. That isn’t one bad decision. It’s three kinds of debt, compounding.

Book a 30-minute meeting See how it compounds ↓

The triangle

Three debts. One system.

Every scaling company builds up three kinds of debt. They don’t stay separate: each one feeds the other two.

Why they compound

It isn’t three problems. It’s one feedback loop.

01

Cultural pressure produces rushed design decisions.

→
02

Rushed design decisions create technical shortcuts.

→
03

Technical shortcuts create more pressure and blame, deepening the cultural debt.

↻ and round again

Fix one side on its own and the other two pull it straight back. That’s why the loop has to be broken at all three points at once.

How the spiral starts

We’ve watched this pattern play out again and again. The names change; the sequence rarely does.

  • Design debt
  • Technical debt
  • Cultural debt
  1. Before funding

  2. The vision

    A founder has a clear idea of what the product should be.

  3. A skeleton, fast

    The first hire is usually a technical one, to sketch out a working skeleton.

    + Technical

  4. Validated among friends

    It’s passed around known circles, and the core functionality holds up.

  5. The first designer arrives

    They find the flows are tangled. Fixing them means changing a skeleton that’s already built.

    + Design+ Technical

  6. The compromise

    The designer is asked to meet engineering halfway. Together they patch up a version for the investor deck.

    + Design+ Technical

  7. After the seed round

  8. Funded, and a product manager joins

    The round closes. The first product manager starts looking under the hood.

  9. The bad news

    They show the founder where the faults are, tell engineering the flows have to change, and tell design to redo work it already did.

    + Cultural

  10. Rework, under pressure

    Design reworks old flows while delivering the next items on the roadmap. Engineering rebuilds what it already shipped.

    + Design+ Technical+ Cultural

  11. Panic

    The founder pushes the team to perform. Burnout sets in, and pressure leads to decisions and judgement calls that add to the frustration.

    + Cultural+ Cultural

  12. ↻ The loop repeats

    Back to rework, with less time and less trust than before, until someone steps in to break it.

Bars show illustrative debt building up at each step.

Breaking the loop

Someone has to stop the spin.

The loop doesn’t slow down on its own. Breaking it takes decisions nobody inside the spiral wants to make:

  1. Go back to basicsRe-anchor on the problem the product exists to solve.
  2. Pause the roadmapStop adding new work on top of broken foundations.
  3. Clean up the flow flawsFix the core journeys properly, once.
  4. Bring the temperature downReset expectations so the team can think again, not just react.
  5. Hold up a mirror to the founderThe hardest step. The conviction that built the company can make its own faults the hardest to see.

Two arms of the framework

Prevent it early. Cure it when it’s here.

Preventive

Build it right from the ground up

We work with startups from the start, so design, technology and culture grow together and the triangle stays small.

  • Founding MVP and early product decisions
  • Flows designed before the skeleton hardens
  • Team habits set before pressure arrives

Curative

Consult and advise when it’s already broken

When the pieces are already broken, we sit down with the team in a consultative, advisory role. It’s more ruthless: sometimes a whole function has to be scrapped so the debt can finally be balanced by revenue.

  • Honest audit across all three sides
  • Hard calls on what to stop, fix or remove
  • A plan the whole team can stand behind
